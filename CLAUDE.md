## What the software should do, in order
1. **Quick math.** Mental arithmetic drills: percentages, fractions to decimals, multiplication shortcuts, growth rates, and back-of-envelope numbers like the ones in banking interviews. Timed, with a running accuracy and speed log.
2. **Poker math.** Outs, equity, pot odds, implied odds, expected value, hand combinations. Show the exact probability next to the shortcut so he learns both.
3. **Sports betting math.** Odds formats (American, decimal, fractional), implied probability, removing the vig, expected value, bankroll sizing and the Kelly criterion. This stage is math practice only. He's under 21 and the tool never places or tracks real-money bets.
4. **Quantitative analysis of financial assets.** Returns, volatility, correlation, Sharpe ratio and drawdown; then building and backtesting simple strategies.

Each stage should work before the next one starts.

## How he learns (build the drills around this)
- **Say it out loud, then check.** His FIR prep method: one line per answer, no notes, about 10 minutes a day.
- **Star what he misses.** A starred item clears after two clean reps in a row. Starred items come back first in the next session. The software should track this automatically.
- **He studies by hand.** Notebook photos may show up as HEIC files; convert them with `sips`.
- **Short sessions he'll actually do** beat long ones he skips.

## Ground rules
- **He has to be able to explain everything he claims.** Quant strategy backtests stay off his resume until he can whiteboard the strategy logic, the backtest method, and the pitfalls (look-ahead bias, overfitting, transaction costs) without help. Stage 4 builds toward that bar. A strategy the code produced that he can't explain doesn't count.
- **Nothing generated for him goes out as his work.** An AI-generated target list once almost ended up in an application as his own work; don't repeat that. The software can be built with Claude, but the understanding has to be his.
- **Plain language.** No jargon without a one-line definition the first time it appears.
