# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-09-30. Auto-drafted from the repository; positions taken from the council session.*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** The ten-minute daily math warm-up for finance recruiting.
**What it does:** Timed drills on the numbers interviewers actually ask (pot odds, multiples, buyout returns, business unit economics), answered out loud then checked. Stars what you miss and brings it back until you own it. Levels move only on typed answers, so the score is honest.
**Product category:** Interview warm-up (not "math app", not "poker trainer").
**Product type:** Web app, no install, works on a phone.
**Business model:** Free. A one-time interview pack and a club license are designed but switched off until retention proves demand.

## Target Audience
**Target users:** Finance and accounting undergraduates in the eight weeks before first-round interviews; first, members of one campus finance club.
**Primary use case:** Not freezing on the mental-math or "teach me something" question in an interview.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Get fast and accurate at interview arithmetic without a calculator.
- Turn a hobby (poker) into an interview answer I can defend.
- Keep a daily habit that fits between classes.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Candidates know the formulas but cannot produce the number out loud, under time, in front of a person.
**Why alternatives fall short:** Zetamac is abstract arithmetic with no finance content. Question banks are read, not practised. Paid prep platforms are built for consulting or quant firms and cost $100+.
**What it costs them:** A blown first round, which in this market is the whole cycle.
**Emotional tension:** Fear of looking slow in the room.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Rocketblocks, FlashQuant (paid, consulting and quant focus); they fall short on finance-specific content and price.
**Secondary:** Zetamac (free arithmetic speed); no finance content, no habit mechanics.
**Indirect:** Doing nothing, reading a PDF the night before, a friend's mock interview.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:** interview-specific numbers; say-it-then-check with a typed honesty check; starred-first repetition; built by a peer who is using it for the same interviews.
**Why customers choose us:** It is the exact question, it takes ten minutes, and it is free.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "I can just use Zetamac." | Zetamac trains speed on abstract numbers. This trains the questions you will be asked, out loud. Use both. |
| "Is the poker stuff relevant?" | Pot odds is a common "teach me something" answer; the drill makes sure you can defend it. Skip the set if you do not play. |
| "Is the betting set gambling?" | Math only. Odds are generated, nothing is recorded, and the same formulas are the poker and finance ones. |

**Anti-persona:** Anyone wanting a poker strategy solver, a betting tool, or a full interview course.

## Customer Language
**Words to use:** warm-up, out loud, ten minutes, the question they ask, honest score.
**Words to avoid:** gamified, AI-powered, platform, mastery journey.

## Brand Voice
**Tone:** Plain, direct, a little dry. A peer, not a coach.
**Personality:** honest, small, useful, unhurried.

## Proof Points
**Metrics:** None yet. The first claimable numbers are sign-ups, five-day returns, and the say-versus-typed gap, computed from the app's own data.

## Goals
**Business goal:** Real users and real retention numbers for the resume; income only if demand appears.
**Conversion action:** Finish a first session; return the next day.
