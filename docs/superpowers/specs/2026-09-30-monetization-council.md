# Council session: should Applied Math become a product?

> Simulated council. Each take is built from the advisor's published frameworks and positions, not their actual review. Date: 2026-09-30.

## The question before the council

Aydin built a ten-minute-a-day math drill app for himself (poker odds, banking interview numbers, betting math, his own businesses' numbers). Should it be opened to other people, and if so, free or paid? The stakes: a few evenings of build time either way; the upside is either a small income or, more likely, a project with real users that he can put on a resume and talk about in interviews. The downside is spending recruiting-season evenings on a product nobody wanted.

**What exists in the market** (from a quick scan, sources at the end): Zetamac is the free standard for raw arithmetic speed. Rocketblocks and FlashQuant charge for structured mental-math prep aimed at consulting and quant firms. Poker odds trainers (Preflop+, Poker Drills) sell at about $10 a month or $80 a year. Nothing found that sits at the intersection: finance-recruiting math with poker and business numbers as the practice material, built around saying the answer out loud.

## Seated: Dunford, Halbert, Hormozi, Godin (council session)

Dunford for positioning (who is this for, against what). Halbert for whether a starving crowd exists. Hormozi for the offer and price, if any. Godin seated as the dissenter: his smallest-viable-audience and permission positions push against "monetize it" and against chasing scale.

---

### April Dunford: position it or it disappears

Her first question: if this app did not exist, what would the customer honestly do? For a finance sophomore prepping for interviews, the answer is Zetamac for a week, a friend's mock interview, and a pile of "400 questions" PDFs. The status quo is the competitor, not Rocketblocks.

What can it do the alternatives cannot? Three things, and they have to be provable: it uses the numbers an interviewer actually asks (EV to equity, multiple to yield, paper LBO returns) rather than abstract arithmetic; it forces the say-it-out-loud rep that interviews require, with a typed check that keeps you honest; and it starts from something the student already likes (poker) instead of a worksheet. Who cares most about that? Not "students". Finance-club sophomores in the eight weeks before first-round interviews. That is the best-fit customer, and the category that makes the strengths obvious is "interview warm-up", not "math app" and not "poker trainer". In a poker-trainer category the app loses to Preflop+ on depth; in a math-app category it loses to Zetamac on speed and price (free).

Her positioning style for this: big fish, small pond. Own "the ten-minute daily warm-up for finance recruiting". Do not try to create a category, and do not try to be a general study tool.

**Bottom line:** Position as the daily warm-up for finance recruiting, for club members in the eight weeks before interviews; anything broader makes the product invisible.

### Gary Halbert: is there a starving crowd?

His only question that matters: who is already spending money on this hunger? Finance students buying Wall Street Oasis guides, Breaking Into Wall Street courses, and Rocketblocks subscriptions are a proven buyer list. They pay $100 to $500 for interview prep. That is a starving crowd, and it is a crowd Aydin is standing inside: he is on the list himself, he knows the club leaders, and he knows the exact week the panic starts.

The poker angle is a grabber, not a market. Poker players who want odds trainers already have them. Do not mail that list. The A-pile message is personal and specific: "I built this for my own FIR interviews; here is the exact question they ask and the drill that got me to answer it in under ten seconds." That lands in the club Slack as a message from a peer, not as a product launch.

His operational point: motion beats meditation. Post it to one club channel this week and watch who opens it twice. If ten people use it twice, there is a market. If nobody does, the plan is done and nothing was lost.

**Bottom line:** The finance-recruiting crowd is starving and Aydin is standing in it; test it with one personal message to one club this week before building anything else.

### Alex Hormozi: the offer math

Value equation. Dream outcome: not freezing on the mental-math question in a Goldman first round. Likelihood: high if the drill really matches the questions, which is provable by the question bank. Time delay: the whole product is ten minutes a day for eight weeks, which is short. Effort: near zero, it is a web page. That is a strong numerator and a tiny denominator, so value is real.

Now the price side, and he would be blunt: the market has a free anchor (Zetamac) and the buyers are students with no money but a large fear. The right money model is not a monthly subscription. It is: give the drills away, make the free version genuinely excellent, and sell one thing at one price to the people who are about to interview: a one-time "interview week" pack at $19 to $29, including the interview-run mode, the full banking set, and a printed index card. One customer at $25 pays for the hosting of a thousand free users. If a club wants it for everyone, sell it to the club at $150 a semester, which is a line item a treasurer can approve without a vote.

He would also say: do not pretend this is a business until the numbers say so. The rule of 100 here is 100 personal messages to students, not 100 ads. And most of the value to Aydin is not the $25; it is being the person who built the thing everyone in the club used.

**Bottom line:** Free core, one $19 to $29 one-time interview pack, optional club license; measure with 100 personal invites, not ads.

### Seth Godin (dissenter): who is it for, and what is it for?

Godin's first move is to reject the framing. The question is not "how to monetize". It is "what change are you trying to make, and for whom?" The answer already exists in this project's own brief: a student who can explain everything he claims. That is the change. The smallest viable audience is not "finance students". It is the twenty people in Aydin's own club who will do ten minutes a day because he does, and who will tell the next twenty.

He would warn, in his own voice, that a pricing page and a launch are the fastest way to turn a generous thing into an average one. Charging students $25 for a drill app in interview week is not remarkable; it is the same as every other prep product. What would be remarkable is a free tool built by a peer, where the honesty check (typed versus said) and the "write it in your own words" rule are the point, and where the product itself is the marketing. Permission comes from being useful first. Would anyone miss it if it were gone? Right now, no. Make that true for twenty people before asking any of them for money.

The strategic-quitting angle from *The Dip*: Aydin's scarce resource is autumn evenings in a recruiting season. Building payment flows, terms of service, and refund handling is the dip of a business he has not decided to be in. The thing he can be best in the world at, this year, is the daily warm-up his club actually does.

**Bottom line:** Free, for the twenty people who will actually use it, with the honesty mechanics as the remarkable part; do not build a paid product until someone asks to pay.

## Where the council disagrees

1. **Charge or not.** Hormozi says price it, because a free product with no buyer never proves value and a $25 pack funds hosting forever. Godin says charging in interview week makes it ordinary and steals attention from being useful. The real trade-off: proof-of-value versus proof-of-use. What settles it: run it free for one recruiting cycle and count people who return five or more days. If that number is above thirty and any of them ask what they can pay, Hormozi is right. If it is under ten, neither price matters.

2. **Who the audience is.** Dunford and Halbert say finance-recruiting students, a proven buyer list. Godin says twenty specific people in one club. This is scale versus resonance. The trade-off resolves by sequence, not by choosing: the club is the first twenty; the buyer list is who they tell. What settles it: whether anyone outside the first club signs up without Aydin messaging them.

3. **Poker as hook or as distraction.** Halbert says do not market to poker players. Dunford says poker belongs in the story ("started from something he liked") but not in the category. Godin would keep it because it makes the thing remarkable and personal. The trade-off: personal story versus category clarity. What settles it: the landing page test. If "interview warm-up" converts better than "poker math for finance", the story stays in the about section.

4. **How much to build before showing anyone.** Hormozi and Halbert: nothing more, send it now. Godin: nothing more, but for the opposite reason (do not build a business). Dunford: at least fix the positioning on the front page first, because a confusing first screen wastes the only launch you get with one club. Settles itself: the front page rewrite is an evening; do it, then send.

## Chair's synthesis

For Aydin's stage (freshman, recruiting season, no money, real time constraints, resume as the prize), the council's weight is clear. Do not build a paid product now. Build the free public version, positioned as the daily warm-up for finance recruiting, launch it by personal message to one club, and instrument it well enough to know whether anyone comes back. The resume line writes itself only if the return numbers are real.

Keep Hormozi's money model on the shelf, fully designed, so it can be switched on in one evening if the signal appears: a one-time $19 to $29 interview pack and a $150-a-semester club license. Keep Godin's warning as the tripwire: the moment building the product replaces using the product, stop.

- **Do:** rewrite the front page to the "interview warm-up" position; add accounts and sync (the phone problem); add the return-visit and retention counters; send one personal message to one club; count returns for four weeks.
- **Tripwire (Godin):** if Aydin's own streak drops below four days a week while building for others, the project has inverted. Stop building and drill.
- **Tripwire (Hormozi):** if 30+ people return five or more days in a cycle and any ask to pay, switch on the pack and the club license.
- **Execute with:** the full-service buildout plan (`2026-09-30-full-service-buildout.md`), `copywriting` for the front page, `analytics` for the retention counters, `launch` for the club message, `pricing` and `offers` only if the second tripwire fires.

## What this means for the resume, honestly

A resume line that says "built a math drill app" is worth little; every student with an AI assistant has one. A line that says "built a free interview warm-up used by N students across M clubs, with 5-day retention of X%" is worth a conversation, and the interviewer will ask how it works, which is the Stage 4 rule from the brief again: only claim what you can whiteboard. The numbers must be real and small numbers stated honestly beat large numbers that cannot be explained.
