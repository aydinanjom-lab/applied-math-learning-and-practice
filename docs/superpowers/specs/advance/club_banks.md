# Napkin: math-forward clubs at IU and a question bank for each

Written Oct 5, 2026. Plan, not code. Five web searches; sources at the end. Some search hits were old bulletin pages, so **check each club is active on beINvolved before pitching it**: names, officers, and meeting times change every year.

Napkin today has 14 sets: interview, poker, quick, banking, accounting, valuation, walks, deals, rates, prob, betting, moose, novyx, energy. "Reuse" below means an existing set; "new" means a set that does not exist yet.

## The clubs, ranked by fit

Fit = how much of what the club's members get asked is already in Napkin. Effort = new drills needed, in evenings.

Not clubs but worth knowing: the **Investment Banking Workshop** and **Investment Management Workshop** are faculty-run, selective, and place most of Kelley's IB and markets hires. They are the highest-value audience, but you approach a director, not a student president, and you should do that only after a student club has used Napkin for a few weeks and you have numbers to show.

## The bank for each club

Each row is the club "pack": sets in the order they would appear in the member's Today queue. New sets get one line of scope and five sample drills.

### 1. FIR
**Pack:** interview, banking, accounting, valuation, walks, quick. Deals unlocks at Solid as now.
Nothing new. Launch here first.

### 2. Banking & Trading Association
**Pack:** banking, valuation, walks, deals, rates, prob, quick, plus **new: Markets in your head.**

*Markets in your head* (S&T and ER first-round questions):
- Basis points: "A 4.25% yield rises 35bp. New yield?" 4.60%.
- FX cross: "EUR/USD 1.10, USD/JPY 150. EUR/JPY?" 165.
- Price move to P&L: "Long 2,000 shares, stock falls $1.35. P&L?" −$2,700.
- EPS growth to price: "EPS grows 12%, P/E stays 18x on $5 EPS. New price?" $100.80.
- Index points to percent: "S&P at 5,400 falls 81 points. Percent?" −1.5%.

### 3. Kelley Undergraduate Research and Trading Club
**Pack:** prob, quick, rates, plus **new: Trading math.**

*Trading math* reuses the betting set's logic in market language, so it never mentions sports or wagers. That also solves the under-21 concern for this audience.
- Edge to EV: "You buy at 48, fair value 50, 1,000 contracts at $1 a point. Expected P&L?" +$2,000.
- Position sizing: "Risk 1% of $50,000 with a $2 stop. Shares?" 250.
- Kelly fraction: "60% to win even money. Kelly fraction?" 20%.
- Implied probability from price: "A binary pays 100 and trades at 37. Implied chance?" 37%.
- Breakeven win rate: "Win $300, lose $200 per trade. Breakeven win rate?" 40%.

### 4. Alternative Investment Group
**Pack:** deals, valuation, rates (options drills), plus **new: Real estate** and **new: Hedge fund exposure.**

*Real estate* (also serves #9):
- Cap rate: "NOI $1.2M, price $20M. Cap rate?" 6%.
- Value from cap rate: "NOI $900K at a 6% cap. Value?" $15M.
- Cash-on-cash: "$2M equity, $160K cash flow after debt. Cash-on-cash?" 8%.
- DSCR: "NOI $1M, debt service $800K. Coverage?" 1.25x.
- LTV and equity: "$30M property, 65% LTV. Equity check?" $10.5M.

*Hedge fund exposure:*
- Gross and net: "$120M long, $80M short, $100M capital. Gross? Net?" 200%, 40%.
- Beta-adjusted net: "Long $50M at beta 1.2, short $40M at beta 0.9. Beta-adjusted net?" $24M.
- Fees: "2 and 20, $100M fund returns 15%. Total fees?" $4.6M: 2 management, plus 20% of the 13 left after it. Conventions vary, so the drill states which one it uses.
- High-water mark: "Down 10% last year, up 15% this year. Performance fee base?" Only the gain above the old high, 3.5%.
- Pair trade P&L: "Long A +4%, short B +1%, $10M each side. P&L?" +$300K.

### 5. Actuarial Club
**Pack:** prob, plus **new: Exam P** and **new: Exam FM.** Members use calculators on the real exams, so Napkin's pitch here is speed and sanity checks, not replacing exam prep. Say that plainly when you pitch it, or it will sound like you don't know the exam.

*Exam P (probability):*
- Binomial: "10 trials, p = 0.2. P(exactly 2)?" 30.2%.
- Poisson: "Mean 3 claims. P(0 claims)?" e^-3 = 5.0%.
- Expectation of a mixture: "70% low risk with mean 100, 30% high risk with mean 400. Mean?" 190.
- Variance of a sum: "Independent, Var 4 and Var 9. Var of the sum?" 13.
- Exponential memorylessness: "Mean life 10 years, survived 5. Expected remaining?" 10.

*Exam FM (financial math):*
- Effective from nominal: "12% compounded monthly. Effective annual?" 12.68%.
- PV of an annuity: "$1,000 a year for 5 years at 5%. PV?" $4,329.
- Loan payment: "$10,000 over 3 years at 6%, annual payments. Payment?" $3,741.
- Bond price: "5-year, 6% annual coupon, yield 5%. Price per 100?" 104.33.
- Continuous rate: "Force of interest 5%. One-year growth factor?" 1.0513.

### 6. Association for Business Analytics
**Pack:** prob, moose, novyx, quick, plus **new: Analytics.**

*Analytics:*
- Conversion lift: "Control 4.0%, test 4.6%. Relative lift?" 15%.
- Sample-size gut check: "Detect a 1-point lift on 5% conversion. Roughly how many per arm?" about 7,600 (rule of thumb: 16 × p(1−p) / d²).
- Confidence interval: "Mean 50, sd 10, n = 100. 95% interval?" 48 to 52.
- Retention to lifetime: "Monthly churn 5%. Average customer lifetime?" 20 months.
- Weighted average: "Region A 300 orders at $40, B 100 at $80. Average order?" $50.

### 7. Data Science Club
**Pack:** prob, analytics (shared with #6), plus **new: ML metrics.**

*ML metrics:*
- Precision and recall: "90 true positives, 10 false positives, 30 false negatives. Precision? Recall?" 90%, 75%.
- F1: "Precision 0.8, recall 0.5. F1?" 0.615.
- Base-rate accuracy: "1% positive class. Accuracy of always guessing negative?" 99%, which is why accuracy misleads.
- Log-odds: "P = 0.8. Odds? Log-odds?" 4, 1.39.
- Expected loss: "False negative costs 10, false positive 1. Flag when P(positive) above?" 1/11, about 9%.

### 8. Undergraduate Financial Services Club
**Pack:** quick, rates, Markets in your head (shared with #2), plus **new: Futures.**
- Contract value: "S&P futures at 5,400, $50 multiplier. Notional?" $270,000.
- Tick P&L: "Long 3 contracts, up 12 points, $50 a point. P&L?" $1,800.
- Margin leverage: "$15,000 margin on $270,000 notional. Leverage?" 18x.
- Basis: "Spot 100, futures 101.50. Basis?" −1.50.
- Fair value: "Spot 100, rate 5%, dividend 2%, one year. Fair futures?" about 103.

### 10. Consulting
**Pack:** quick, moose (margin, break-even), prob (market sizing drill), plus **new: Case math.**
- Profit tree: "Revenue up 10%, costs up 15%, margin was 20%. New margin?" 16.4%.
- Breakeven volume: "Fixed $500K, price $50, variable $30. Units?" 25,000.
- Market share: "Market $2B growing 5%, client $200M growing 2%. Share next year?" 9.7%.
- Payback: "$1.2M investment saves $40K a month. Payback?" 30 months.
- Percent of percent: "40% of customers, 25% of those convert. Share of total?" 10%.

## What tailoring per club needs in the app

Today a club only changes two things: whether the betting set is hidden, and which set a club session uses. A real pack needs three small changes:

1. **A `sets` list on the club.** The leader picks the pack when creating the club, starting from a preset like "FIR" or "Trading club". One column in `cohorts`, one picker on the leader page.
2. **Members' Today queue includes the club's sets.** It merges with their own priorities, club sets first for the first two weeks. This is a change to `priorities.js` with tests.
3. **The club-session picker lists only the pack.** That is a filter on the existing dropdown.

That is about one evening plus a short SQL addition, separate from the new sets.

## Order I would do it in

1. Launch FIR on the existing bank. Do not build anything new first: you need two weeks of real use to know what to build.
2. Add the club `sets` list (one evening). It costs little and every later club needs it.
3. Build **Markets in your head** and **Trading math** (two evenings). Together with the existing sets they cover the Banking & Trading Association and the Trading Club, which are the next two easiest yeses.
4. Build **Real estate** (one evening). It covers AIG and the Real Estate Club.
5. Build **Exam P / Exam FM** only if the Actuarial Club's president says yes. It's the most work and the narrowest audience.
6. Build **Analytics** and **ML metrics** only if you reach those clubs. They're the furthest from your own recruiting, so you get the least interview value from building them.

## Ground rule from the brief

You have to be able to explain every new set before it ships to a club. For the sets far from your own prep (Exam FM, ML metrics), that means one of two things. Either you learn them yourself first, or you have a member of that club check the drills before launch. Shipping a set to the Actuarial Club with a wrong annuity formula would cost you that club, and probably the next one too.
